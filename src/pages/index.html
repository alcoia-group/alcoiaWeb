Working memory holds a handful of items at once. When a sentence keeps you waiting for its verb, or a paragraph introduces four new terms in a row, that budget runs out before the meaning does, and you keep scrolling anyway.

Reading researchers call the fix retrieval practice: being asked to recall something, rather than being shown it again, is what moves it into memory you can actually use later.

That is the whole mechanism. Not a summary. Not a highlight. A short question, answered in your own words, about the sentence you just read.

What happens instead

You read it. alcoia checks that it landed

The text stays yours to read. alcoia watches pace and re-reading in the browser, and when a passage looks like it slipped past, it asks one short question about that exact sentence, not a topic or summary. Answering right ends it. Answering wrong is when an explanation appears.

How it works

Three honestly labelled stages

01

It watches how you read

Pace against the difficulty of the paragraph, against your own usual rate, scrolling back to re-read, selecting or copying, leaving the tab. No camera needed. This is the primary and default path.

02

It notices when you slow down

Against the difficulty of what you're reading, against your own baseline pace, and against re-reading patterns. These signals combine to spot where comprehension is likely to slip.

03

It asks

A short question about what you just read, with the sentence containing the answer quoted underneath. Wrong, it explains. Right, it gets out of the way. At most one interruption every three minutes, five a session.

Research

Built on other people's evidence, not ours yet

Built on reading research from Rayner (1998), Just & Carpenter (1980) and D'Mello et al. (2016), whose randomised trial found that interrupting a drifting reader with just-in-time questions recovered comprehension losses (d = 0.47).

No accuracy figure for alcoia itself is published anywhere on this site. There has been no real-participant evaluation yet, and a number with nothing real behind it is worse than no number at all.

Questions

Do I need a webcam?

No. Reading detection works with browser telemetry alone. The camera is optional and off by default.

What's the difference between a summary and a question?

A summary is something you're shown. A question is something you have to answer using the text you just read. That retrieval is what creates real retention.

When does Alcoïa interrupt?

Only when your reading pattern suggests something didn't stick. If you're reading smoothly at an appropriate pace, Alcoïa stays out of the way.

Does Alcoïa share my data?

No. Everything stays on your device or in your private account. You can delete it anytime. For classrooms, you choose what to share with instructors.
